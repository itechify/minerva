# The Pickup Log is counted by its stack's shift

The Pickup Log is the only live source for mesos and Items (ADR 0005), and the only place the game says which EXP was the player's own and which came from a party. Its lines are hard to count. Two recordings at 10 Hz (2026-10-07 solo, 2026-10-25 partied on The Murky Swamp I, #21) show how the log behaves:

- Lines are right-aligned, 22 px apart at the samples' UI Size, ending at client x=1908 of a 1920×1200 client area, the newest on a fixed bottom row (client y=960).
- A new line pushes every older one up exactly one row between two frames. It never slides. It is at full brightness on its first frame. A 7th line takes the oldest away in the same frame, and at most 6 show.
- Each line fades on its own clock, at a speed that varies (1.4 to 2.7 s to vanish while partied and busy, about 5.5 s solo), so a line's brightness doesn't tell its age.
- Lines arrive in bursts: 58% of the frames that bring lines bring 2 or more, an EXP line and its `Bonus EXP for PARTY` line always together, and now and then 6 or more at once, more than the log can show.
- The same line again is routine (`You received EXP (+14)` and `Bonus EXP for PARTY (+1)` three times running, `You have gained mesos (+29)` twice), so a line's text doesn't tell it from one already counted.
- The log is drawn under the game world: Mobs, platforms, drops, damage numbers, skill effects and name tags cover lines, a whole log for 3 or 4 frames.
- `You received EXP` is white for the player's own kill and yellow for a share of a partymate's. While partied, every kill brings a `Bonus EXP for PARTY` line, yellow.
- An Item's name too long for the line is cut short: `Arrows for Cros... x21 earned. (Use)`. An inventory tab that's full shows no line, because the game doesn't pick the Item up.

So the reader counts lines by how far the stack moved, not by what they say, and never counts a line it isn't sure of.

## The decision

- **It finds the log itself**, as every reader does (ADRs 0014, 0016, 0017, 0021), with no marking step: in a band at the bottom-right of the client area, sized by the UI Size the Status Bar measures (ADR 0020), it looks for right-aligned lines 22 px apart at the samples' size, and reads them there. The log is **anchored to the client area's right edge**: its lines end a fixed small distance from it (11 to 12 px at the samples' size, in both recordings and the Bug Report below), so only a stack of closing parentheses within 2 to 16 px of the band's right edge, at the Status Bar's UI Size (passed with the capture, not measured again from the lines) within 15%, is a candidate. A first version searched the whole band, and a Bug Report (0.15.0, 2026-10-26) showed it locking onto rock and cloud textures at three wrong sizes, and keeping them, so no arrival was ever read in five minutes. The located log is then **drawn at the UI Size the Status Bar measured**, to the pixel, not at a size measured from its own parentheses: over a bright background a parenthesis is a pixel or two short and two rows' pitch a pixel off, which put the size at 0.88 to 0.95 of the true 1 in a Bug Report (0.15.0, 2026-10-26 09:03), shrinking every line before it was read and keeping the stack's rows from matching. **At a UI Size other than the samples'** the log is located on the capture scaled to the samples' size (ADR 0020), as every reader reads, and the layout brought back (only a smaller one, 0.64, is in the footage; a larger one is scaled down, and takes the same path): the game draws its text at 0.64 (a 1280×720 client area, a Status Bar 35 px high) with strokes one pixel wide, joined only at their corners, so the colour mask's 4-connected pieces were no parenthesis at all and a Bug Report (0.15.0, 2026-10-26 14:41) found nothing in 6,652 captures. A scaled-up parenthesis is looser: its height may be 0.6 to 1.4 of the true one, and one side of its bulge is enough, the right margin, the alignment and the confirmation below standing guard.
- **A located log is confirmed before it is trusted.** It counts nothing, and reports nothing, until one of its rows reads as a known line kind (EXP, mesos, an Item), though it is followed meanwhile so that lines arriving as it is confirmed are counted. A located layout none of whose rows has read for 3 s is a miss: it is dropped and searched for again, so a lookalike is never kept. A confirmed log that goes empty is kept.
- **Counted by the shift.** Each capture's lines are matched to the last capture's by their shapes, not their brightness. The new lines are the bottom ones, as many as the stack moved, and only they are read: they are at full brightness, the easiest to read. A line's ink is the white or yellow pixels with a shadow just below and right of them: a bright pixel without one is the world behind the line (a yellow-green leaf, a white sky), which the same colour test took for ink. The recogniser's usual slips on the game's fixed words are read through, never a different kind of line: `earned` within two letters, the `x` before the quantity as another mark, as nothing, or run into the name (`Liquidx1`), the tab name within one letter, and `+` as `*`. The quantity is always the last word's digits, so a name that ends in a digit is never one. Where more than one shift fits, the text and colour decide (a white `(+22)` where a yellow `(+21)` stood), then brightness (a line at full brightness where a faded one stood is new), and otherwise the smallest shift that fits. Six new rows count as six: any more were never on the screen. **At a UI Size other than the samples'**, three things change, because the glyphs are resampled (blurred, when scaled up from a smaller one, as in the footage) and a pixel off: the last 45 columns of two rows need to agree on 65%, not 80%, to be one line; only a full-brightness row is new (a readable row beside one that matched no longer stands in for it), and a fresh line that fades below readable is still the same line while its shape matches; and a line is read three ways (as is, with its ink weighed another way, and cut at a threshold) and counts only when all three parse and agree on its kind and figures, else it is `Unreadable`: in the footage a `+39` read as `+30` for the world a leaf or a beam added behind it, so a line may read as fewer, never as another. A blurred `+` often reads as two marks (`(??58;`), which only that agreement makes safe to accept.
- **A new line is followed and read again.** A line stays on screen for 1.5 to 5 s, and the first read of it is the one the world behind it spoils most (in a Bug Report, 0.15.0, 2026-10-26 23:05, 349 of the 18 minutes' arrivals were `Unreadable`, 36% of them, mostly single new lines). So a line is still counted exactly once, by the shift, but what it reads as is decided by a vote: it is read again at each of the next captures while it is at full brightness, up to 5 reads or 500 ms, and no more once 3 reads are identical, or when it fades, is shifted out of the stack or is covered. The kind and figures with most of the reads that parsed win (a tie between two is `Unreadable`, and a read that is the only one that parsed stands, as a line read once did); an Item's name is the one read most often among them. A line whose reads never parse stays `Unreadable`. The reading where the vote ends carries the line, so a Bug Report's replay needs no pixels, and a line waits for the older ones before it, so lines come in the order they arrived. It comes at most half a second after the capture that brought it (the Session counts it then), and one still being read when the stream ends, or the log is lost, is not counted: fewer, never more. At the samples' size, which reads well at the first capture, the vote only adds the agreement of the later reads; at another, a line's three reads at a capture must still agree, and the vote is over the captures.
- **Never overcount.** A pickup it can't be sure of isn't counted, so a total can be low, never high. The Grind Tracker says the figures come from the Pickup Log and may undercount (ADR 0005).
- **A covered capture is skipped.** One whose rows can't be read is left out, and the next clean capture is matched to the last clean one: a line lasts at least about 1.4 s and the stack holds 6, so a cover loses nothing unless more than 6 lines arrive under it. A cover is what would otherwise fake a burst. A stack that matches nothing of the last clean one for 3 s is not a cover that will lift but one that changed (a moving background, a skill effect's beam, a line fading as it was compared): the next capture is the new baseline, and what arrived meanwhile is missed rather than guessed at. Without it a reader that read nothing for 8 s at 0.64 was waiting for a stack that had gone.
- **10 times a second, on a thread of its own.** At 10 Hz the footage loses lines only to bursts no rate would catch. The `reader` thread reads the Minimap Title, the Status Bar and the Deal Finder's whole client area in turn, and a tick held up there would lose a burst. The capture is the log's band alone, and only new lines are read.
- **Only while it's needed:** while the Grind Tracker is on, a Character is on the Status Bar, and the overlay isn't hidden, as for the EXP figures.
- **Every line is recorded**, of whichever kind: the player's own EXP, a party share, the party bonus, mesos, and Items with their quantity and tab. EXP's amount still comes from the EXP bar (ADR 0005); its lines give the EXP Split and tell party play from solo.
- **Items are named from OSMS's item files** (ADR 0010), with the matcher Item Lookup uses. A name cut short is matched by what it starts with, its tab narrowing the choice. One that starts more than one Item's name is tallied as the game shows it (`Arrows for Cros...`), and one that matches no Item is tallied as read once the same text has been read on two lines, so a single misread never makes an Item of its own. A name the whole set can't place is also tried against the names of the Items filed under the line's own tab alone, where it counts if it is within 75% (edits over length) of one name and two edits nearer to it than to any other, never one of two: a name read a few glyphs wrong (`Equisny Liq?id` for `Squishy Liquid`) still names its Item. The whole set asks for 85%. **The recogniser's usual slips on this font are cheap in that comparison** (`pickup_slips.rs`): a `D` read as `Ci`, `Li` or `[i`, a `B` as `Bl`, `El` or `A`, a `V` at the name's start lost or read as `M`, `W` or `t`, a `y` as a `v`, an `n` as `r` or `ri`, a `?` for any one glyph, and the few others the Bug Report of 0.15.0, 2026-10-26 23:05 shows in more than one read, each costing 0.4 of an edit. In that report a quest picked up 100 Very Dry Branches: 57 lines read as the Item, 47 were tallied, and the 10 others read as `Very Cir Aranch`, `(ery [Ciry Elranch`, `Very Ciry Elrarich` and the like, matching nothing at 75% as plain edits. A read now goes to the closest Item of its tab if it is within 75% by that cost and 1.8 edits nearer than the next; otherwise the plain rule above still applies. A read equally close to two Items stays as read, and nothing of another tab is ever chosen. Replayed, the report's 57 lines are all tallied as Very Dry Branch (47 before), and 15 of its 230 Item lines in all change Item, every one from "tallied as read, or once read twice" to an Item, none the other way. The cost is that a misread of one Item can be named as a similar one whose slips it also fits, so **a single Item's count can occasionally be a little high; the total of Items never is**, since the lines counted are the stack's shift, which this changes in nothing (the FAQ says so).

## Considered options

- **The text of each line as its identity**, counting a line not seen before. The same line again is routine, so it would count three `(+14)` lines as one.
- **A line's brightness as its age.** Lines fade at speeds that vary, by how busy the log is, so two lines that arrived together can be far apart in brightness a second later.
- **A best guess where the shift is unclear**, which would come closer on average. A guess can overcount, and a figure that may be high can't say what it is.
- **A Pickup Log Region the player marks**, as #14 planned. No other reader has a marking step now (ADR 0021), and the log's lines are their own anchor.
- **The `reader` thread at 10 Hz**, with the other readers. Simpler, but a busy tick loses a burst.
- **A slower rate, such as 5 Hz.** Half the captures, but twice as many lines arrive between two of them, and a burst of more than 6 loses lines.

## Consequences

- Before the log has been located since a reset, finding no lines cannot prove it is empty: a Mob may cover it. The first clean sighting establishes a baseline, so its lines may be missed rather than counted twice. A Bug Report keeps no crop for each capture of an unlocated empty log, a common case; it keeps one when previously seen lines vanish. A reader that has found nothing for 30 s (ADR 0020's last consequence) has a whole capture of its band kept, and not another for 5 minutes, so a log that is never found can be looked for in the report: the one of 0.15.0 had none of 6,652.
- Mesos come first, on the Grind Tracker's Session: the Session's total and mesos/h, with a ⓘ saying where they come from. The Item tally, in the same Loot section (collapsed at first, with the totals on its header), comes next on the same reader.
- Party play is known from the log: any 5 minutes with Party EXP in them are party. That lets each map have a Solo Best and a Party Best. ADR 0015 ruled out party tags on the Map Leaderboards because Minerva couldn't tell party play from the screen; it now can, and that decision is reopened in an issue of its own.
- A best with no known kind is a Party Best, everywhere: the saved bests file's move to version 3 marks every older best a Party Best (a version 3 file keeps its kinds), and the site's Worker reads an upload entry with no `party` as one, as its migration marks the rows from before. The kind is the window's alone: the player can't move a Visit between the Solo and Party Bests (it can still be moved to another Character), because a Visit moved by hand would race and rank as a figure no play of that kind set. The cost is that a player's earlier bests race as Party Bests, so their Solo Best on a map starts empty until they set a new one.
- Our windows over the log hide it from the reader, as from every reader. The player may put one there; while one has covered it for more than a few seconds, the Grind Tracker says the Pickup Log is covered.
- A Bug Report writes down each capture's reading (the lines found, the shift taken, the lines read, whether shapes alone left more than one shift, and whether it had to guess: more than one still fitted after colour and brightness, and the smallest was taken), and keeps a crop of the log only for a capture where it guessed, lost previously seen lines, or read a line it couldn't, and at most one in 10 s of capture time for each of those causes, so a busy log is a handful of crops, not the thousands that crowded the EXP figures out of a report of 0.15.0 (3,850 of 3,928, each for a choice between shapes). Those become samples.
- The samples in `samples/pickup-log` are short sequences of 10 Hz crops, each with a list of the lines that arrived, checked by eye: repeated lines, a burst of 6 or more, a covered log, the first lines into an empty one, names cut short, over more than one background. None shows another player's name: a name tag in one is painted out.
- The samples in `samples/pickup-log-720p` are the game's own drawing at a UI Size of 0.64 (a 1280×720 client area), in short clips fed as the real band is. Read from a cold start, they count the lines that arrived, each within half a second of the frame that brought it or not at all, and no mesos or Items that did not: of the 30 lines that arrived in them, 19 read whole (14 before a line was read again), and every other one arrived as `Unreadable` or was missed under a cover or before the log was found (the clips say which). The log is found in 101 of the 149 one-second windows of the recording read from a cold start, nearly all of the others holding no fresh line. Read warm, over the recording's whole 150 s, the stack's shifts count 138 lines at 10 Hz and again at both phases of 5 Hz, and of the 138 the reader reads 124 whole (58 EXP, 32 mesos, 34 Items; 95 when a line was read only as it arrived), none as another: EXP is only `+39` or `+58`, and mesos only amounts the recording shows. The three reads cost recall, as a line is read only when none of them doubts it; two reads in three read 23 of the clips' 30 but one `+39` as `+30`.
- Not yet sampled: a UI Size above the samples' (it takes the same path, scaled down, and the existing clips scaled to 1.45 and 1.9 read fewer, never another), and quest Items.
