# Design System: Minerva

## Overview

**Creative North Star: "The Window the Game Forgot to Ship"**

The overlay is one more of the game's windows laid over the screen, built from the two it sits beside. From the Mini Map it takes the light grey window bar with the window's name and small bright blue buttons, and the sky-blue title panel with the map's Map Mark beside the Street Name over the Map Name in light lettering outlined in blue. From the Quest Helper it takes the see-through periwinkle body with near-black text. The minimap's legend supplies the glyphs: blue portal rings, red mob dots, and the player's yellow dot. The pixel-art owl, the app icon, is the window's mark. The game supplies the materials and one signature move; organisation, hierarchy and density come from a working tool: EXP pace first when it is tracked, then the map and its hidden exits, then the way to the Hunting Target, then the mobs, then the credit. It is not a screenshot of a game window.

It is light because the game's windows are light, see-through because the game's windows over the screen are, and dense because it is read in glances between attacks. Every state is drawn in the same window. Doubt and problems get the game's orange, and nothing else does. The confirmed rejection is the generic dark dev-tools card with a stats table on top, which this world replaced. Setup is a window on the desktop rather than over the game: it keeps an opaque lavender body, navy ink and steel buttons under the same grey window bar, and shows a recognised map exactly as the overlay's title panel will. The project's site is that same opaque window opened wide in the browser: a home page that shows what Minerva does a feature at a time, the Map Leaderboards, where players' Personal Bests stand side by side map by map, and a FAQ that answers everything else, its raised tabs its pages.

Every window takes the pointer. The overlay is always clickable, but a click never takes the keyboard from the game except in the search view, where the query field needs it. The Settings window takes it as it opens, and again on a Change, where a hotkey's new keys are pressed, or on the Deal Margin field; a click back in the game gives it back, and the window stays open. The panel is dragged by its window bar or title panel when it sits inside the game or is detached, and resized by its sides or bottom corners, width only. Its buttons and setup's buttons carry the game's hover, pressed and keyboard-focus states; list rows in the search view take a faint hover tint. Its height follows its content. Motion answers the player (see Motion): the Mark Card's close-up dives from the map to a portal's or spawn point's spot as the pointer comes to rest on it, while what the game changes, a new map or an EXP reading (and with it the Grind Tracker's Pace Trace and forecast), changes at once, as the game's own windows do. On the site two things move with the scroll, as the game's minimap dot moves only as the character walks: the site minimap's yellow dot and the site hero's film. One moves with a click: a map's Map Leaderboard switching Level Band, its Characters in both bands sliding to their new rank and the lanes regrowing. With reduced motion nothing moves: the close-up opens on the spot, the dot steps from portal to portal, the hero is its still shot and the band switches at once.

**Key Characteristics:**
- The Mini Map's light grey window bar and sky-blue title panel over the Quest Helper's see-through periwinkle body, in a 4px-cornered frame.
- The Map Mark beside Street Name over Map Name in light lettering outlined in blue, as the Mini Map draws its title; the same mark at half size beside a map's name wherever else it shows.
- Three icon vocabularies only: minimap glyphs (portal ring, mob dot, you dot and the you yellow star, and on the site's Map Leaderboards the crowns on a band's top 3) and the Map Preview's rings, route rings and teleport arrows in CSS and SVG, drawn line icons on the game's small blue buttons, and the pixel-art owl as the window's mark. The Map Marks, and on the site the job icons, are the game's own art, not a vocabulary of ours.
- Near-black ink with a faint light edge on the see-through body; navy ink on setup's opaque lavender.
- Game orange appears only for doubt and problems.
- NEXON's Maplestory typeface throughout, Light and Bold; numeric columns right-aligned.
- Buttons in every window have the game's hover, pressed and focus states. Motion answers the player, in one exponential ease-out, and never runs on a timer: in the panel, the Mark Card's close-up diving to the spot; on the site, the minimap's dot and the hero's film, both moved by the scroll, and a Map Leaderboard's band switch, moved by the click. With reduced motion nothing moves.

## Colors

Cool periwinkles, sky blue and light grey from the game's own windows, near-black and navy inks, three minimap accents (portal blue, mob red, you yellow), two more ring colours for the Map Preview (teleport green, unknown grey), a route magenta for the way to the Hunting Target, a reserved game orange, the game's bright blue for overlay buttons, a deep steel for setup's, and the game's EXP lime for its EXP gauge alone.

### Primary
- **Portal Ring Blue** (portal): the minimap's portal ring. The section mark for the Map (over Portal Ring Fill, portal-fill), and the ring of every portal to another map, Doors included, on the Map Preview and on its Hidden Portal's row.

### Secondary
- **Game Orange** (orange, with orange-deep for edges and text outlines): the game's small orange button. The dot in problem notes (overlay, setup and the Grind Tracker's "Can't read the EXP bar"), in the Settings window's line under keys it refused, and the collapsed icon's alert dot. The "Not sure" chip is the game's orange button itself, a gradient from Doubt Gold (doubt-top) to Doubt Amber (doubt-base). Problem notes sit on Problem Cream (problem-bg), edged in Problem Amber (problem-edge), with Problem Brown text (problem-ink). The "paused" tag in the Grind Tracker borrows that cream, amber and brown, without the orange, because held figures are a state to notice; so do the Settings window's "Taken" and "Invalid" tags, and the keys it refused, set in a cream chip until others are pressed, and in the Personal Bests window a button asking before it does what can't be undone ("Remove?", the switch asking).

### Tertiary
- **Mob Dot Red** (mob, edged by mob-edge): the minimap's mob dot. The section mark for Mobs, the bullet on each mob row and on a spawn point's card, and every Spawn Point on the Map Preview and in the close-up.
- **Party Gold** (`--party: #a97800`, `--party-ink: #7a5a00`): Party EXP's part of the EXP Split and the two-figure party mark beside Best. The darker ink writes small Party labels in the Personal Bests window and on the site's entries, so the mark remains legible on lavender. It identifies party play, alongside the existing figure hierarchy.
- **You Yellow** (you, lit by you-light, edged by you-edge): the minimap's dot for the player's own character. It marks the Hunting Target's arrival, "You're here", as a 10px dot with a you-light highlight up and to the left (and the map the player is on in the Personal Bests window the same way, and on its lane, 8px, the Visit under way's best), and one other thing about the player, their best: the Grind Tracker's star on Last 5 min while it is a new best on the map (and the Personal Bests window's star on their best of all maps, and on the site's Map Leaderboards the star before their entry the link names), an 11px inline SVG star filled You Yellow, lit by a you-light highlight up and to the left, and ringed in you-edge. Kept clear of Game Orange: a pale yellow with a dark gold edge, never a chip or a note.
- **Crown Gold, Crown Silver and Crown Bronze** (crown-gold, lit from `#ffe58a` at the top down to `#d99a00`, ringed in crown-edge; crown-silver, from `#f4f6fa` down to `#7e8898`, ringed in crown-silver-edge; crown-bronze, from `#f7d6b6` down to `#a5602b`, ringed in crown-bronze-edge): the crown before the rank of a band's #1, #2 and #3, on a map's page and on a Character's page, each metal lit at its top as a medal is (the gold is the Map Leaderboards drawing's). They mark a place on the board, which You Yellow can't: You Yellow is the player's own, and on a map's page still stars the linked Character's entry, whatever its crown. Only ever the crown's shape, 14px, never a chip, a note, a fill or text, so gold reads as neither doubt nor a Deal Badge's gold, and bronze not as Game Orange.
- **Teleport Green** (teleport): the Map Preview's green for a teleport within the map, OSMS Data Explorer's meaning in a deeper shade. The ring of a portal that leads elsewhere on the same map, its dashed arrow to where it lands, and the row ring of such a Hidden Portal. The panel's one true green, kept apart from EXP Lime.

### Named Rules
**The Orange Means Doubt Rule.** Game orange marks only uncertainty and problems: the "Not sure" chip, problem notes, the collapsed alert dot. A confirmed state never uses it, so orange can't become decoration. Search tiers are told apart by weight and ink, never by orange.

**The Minimap Legend Rule.** Portal blue, mob red and you yellow come from the minimap's legend and keep that meaning. Blue is for portals, red is for mobs (the mob rows' dots, and the Map Preview's dot on every Spawn Point, as the minimap dots mobs) and yellow is for where the player is, with no other uses but one: you yellow also draws a best (the star for the Grind Tracker's new best, the Personal Bests window's best of all maps and, on the site's Map Leaderboards, the entry a link from the Personal Bests window names, and on the Pace Trace, the Best's pace in you-edge gold ending in a best's star, the lead lit over it and the split against it), since all are about the player. The Map Preview adds two ring colours from OSMS Data Explorer, each with one meaning: Teleport Green for a portal within the map and its arrow, Unknown Grey for a portal to a map not in the data. Blue stays the ring of a portal to another map, Doors included; green is never a tag. Route Magenta is the panel's own and means one thing, the way to the Hunting Target or the Destination: the wide ring around a portal the route takes next, the Navigation section's mark, and the ring at a Destination's route's end.

**The EXP Lime Rule.** The lime of the game's EXP gauge draws EXP and nothing else: the EXP bar's reading in the Grind Tracker, a map's best EXP/h on its lane in the Personal Bests window (each Visit's on its own), and each entry's EXP/h on its lane on the site's Map Leaderboards. Not progress in general, not a rank, and never a highlight: the linked Character's lane is lime like everyone's, and You Yellow's star marks it. The ground a death took back, the forecast's notches, a lane's notches for its other Visits and a ruler's notches are the gauge's frame grey, never lime: they aren't the reading.

**The Item Grade Rule.** A deal is graded as the game grades its items, blue, then purple, then gold, which players already read as good, better, best: a good deal is the deep steel button, a great one (20% or more below the Deal Line) Epic Purple (`#8456d1` to `#6435b0`, edged `#3f1f7a`, white label), a steal (40% or more) Unique Gold (`#fff1a8` through `#ffd83a` to `#f2bd12`, edged `#8a6400`, dark brown `#3d2a00` ink, its gauge brown). Those colours mean a deal's size on a Deal Badge and nothing else. The gold is a clear yellow with dark ink and a bright top shine, never the doubt chip's amber with white lettering, so it can't be read as doubt.

**The Over the Game, See-Through Rule.** A window laid over the game is see-through: the overlay on the Quest Helper's glass. A window on the desktop (setup) is opaque. Same family, different footing.

**The Two Inks Rule.** Text on the see-through glass is the Quest Helper's near-black Panel Ink; text on lavender is navy Ink. Never mix them in one window. The panel sets its own `color`: inheriting the body's would carry navy into every figure that doesn't name its ink.

## Typography

**Display Font:** Maplestory (NEXON's typeface, bundled in `assets/fonts`; see its NOTICE), falling back to Arial, "Segoe UI", system-ui, sans-serif, as the shared `--font`
**Body Font:** the same family
**Label/Mono Font:** none distinct; Maplestory's figures are proportional (it has no tabular figures)

**Character:** Every window is set in Maplestory, NEXON's own rounded sans for the game (chosen by the user in October 2026), so the text reads as the game's. It comes in two weights, declared as one family in `src/fonts.css`: Light answers weights up to 500, so regular text (400) is Light, and Bold answers 600 and up. Weight (Bold against Light) and colour carry the hierarchy, not a second family. It loads with `font-display: block`, bundled with the app, so text never draws in Arial first and changes width; the overlay measures its height again once it has loaded. It lacks the en dash, em dash and minus sign, which come from Arial. Light is thinner than the game draws its window text over a busy scene, so the panel gives all its text a 0.3px stroke in the text's own colour (`-webkit-text-stroke-width: var(--text-weight-stroke)`); more clogs the Bold figures. The outlined title lettering and the doubt chip, whose outlines are their weight, take none. Maplestory sets wider than Arial: a long Map Name takes its second line sooner, and long single-line facts truncate sooner.

**The Right-Aligned Figures Rule.** Numeric columns are right-aligned so EXP, HP, rates and share line up at their ends from one glance to the next. Maplestory's figures are proportional and it has no tabular ones; the body still asks for `tabular-nums`, which the Arial fallback honours.

**The Chrome, Not Lift Rule.** Depth is drawn the way the game draws it, with highlights, bevels and hairlines. Nothing floats on a blurred drop shadow.

## Motion

The game's windows mostly hold still, and the panel sits in the corner of the player's eye between attacks, where anything that travels reads as an alert. So a window moves where it answers the player, to show where something they reached for comes from, and holds still where the game changes it.

- **Answering the pointer:** what the player does with the pointer may move, spatially: a card opening out of what it describes, a zoom, a fold. 150–400ms on one exponential ease-out (`1 − 2^(−10t)`, scaled to land exactly, where script drives it; `cubic-bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1)` in CSS), from a first frame already readable, so nothing waits on a motion to be read. A zoom moves evenly in scale (interpolated in log space), not in size, and what it shows keeps its own size.
- **What the game changes** (a new map, an EXP reading, a note, the Hunting Target's arrival) changes at once, or at most cross-fades in 150ms or less: never a slide, a count-up or a bounce. The Grind Tracker's EXP gauge stays un-eased, as the game's own is, and so do its Pace Trace and forecast, which move only as the EXP does.
- **Never:** loops, pulses, shimmer, motion on a timer, or anything that overshoots. The "Not sure" chip and the alert dot hold still: they are states, not alarms.
- **Reduced motion:** with `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`, everything is at once, in its final state.
- **One moment at a time:** a window has one authored motion where it matters, not an entrance on every part.

In the windows those moments are, so far, the Mark Card's close-up diving to a portal's or spawn point's spot (see Mark Card), and the Item Card unfolding out of the Deal Badge it was opened from (see Deal Badges). On the site, the minimap's dot and the hero's film move only with the scroll, and two things answer a click: a map's Map Leaderboard switching Level Band, and a Character's name followed to their page.

- **The band morph (one of the site's two click-answered motions):** a click on a Level Band (or Back and Forward between bands) switches the page in place, a same-document View Transition, its address pushed so the link still shares. The rows of Characters with an entry in both bands slide from their old rank to their new one, the linked Character's below the top 50 too, each row by a name of its own; the rest of the table cross-fades, rows going and coming; and every lane regrows from the left on the new band's scale. All of it in 300ms on the exponential ease-out, moving only `transform` and `opacity`, the head that sticks at the top held over the rows going by under it. A row stays readable as it slides, and nothing waits on the motion: the new band is the page as soon as it is drawn. Arriving at a page, a deep link included, never moves (but for the seam, which answers the click that left the page before): it opens on the band, scrolled to the linked entry. A browser without View Transitions, reduced motion, or a swipe back the browser draws itself switches at once, nothing moving; with script off the bands are plain links.
- **The seam (name to Character page):** a Character's name clicked anywhere on the Map Leaderboards (a band's row, Where to train's #1, Latest) glides from where it was into the Character page's heading, growing to Site Title, while the page under it cross-fades in 200ms and the window's head settles from where it stuck to its place: a cross-document View Transition (`site/seams.html`, written into the head of the Map Leaderboards' pages and the Character page, so it is in place before their first paint). 320ms on the exponential ease-out, moving only `transform` and `opacity`; the heading is as wide as its words while it lands, so they scale as words. It answers the click and nothing else: a tab, a map, the way back, and Back and Forward are plain navigations, as is all of it in a browser without cross-document View Transitions (Firefox) or with reduced motion. Only what a page draws before its first paint can take part, so the seam lands on the heading (written from the address before any script) and never on what the API fills in.

### Named Rules
**The Motion Answers Rule.** A window moves only to show the player where something they reached for comes from. What the game changes, changes in place.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do:
- **Do** build new overlay states from the game's window materials: the grey window bar, the Mini Map's sky-blue title panel, the Quest Helper's see-through glass with near-black text, and the game's bright blue buttons.
- **Do** keep setup on its opaque lavender with navy ink and steel buttons under the same grey window bar.
- **Do** use a see-through body for windows over the game and an opaque one for windows on the desktop.
- **Do** reserve game orange for doubt and problems, and pair it with words and a shape (chip, dot, bordered note) so the state never relies on colour alone.
- **Do** draw icons as minimap glyphs (portal ring, mob dot, you dot and the you yellow star) and the Map Preview's rings and arrows in CSS or inline SVG (on a canvas in the Mark Card's close-up), or as 2-stroke line icons on the game's small buttons; the pixel-art owl is the only raster and marks the window (on the site, the logo heads the hero too, and the drawn titles head the FAQ and the Map Leaderboards, the group owls the FAQ's groups, and the owl lost a page with nothing to show).
- **Do** keep numeric columns right-aligned.
- **Do** let notes and the Map Name wrap, and truncate single-line facts with an ellipsis.
- **Do** keep the Street Name above the Map Name wherever a map is shown as the overlay shows it.
- **Do** draw EXP as the game's own EXP gauge, in EXP Lime: the EXP bar's reading, and a best's EXP/h on its lane. Nothing else in that lime.
- **Do** give every button, in every window, hover, pressed and a 2px Edit Blue `:focus-visible` ring offset 2px: game blue on the overlay's window bars, light steel in its body, deep steel for setup's step forward and light steel beside it.
- **Do** mark a selected row with Selection Periwinkle and its inset edge, and set a list into the body as an inset box.
- **Do** set a window read at rest in the browser (the site) in setup's opaque language, framed in Window Edge on Quest Helper Glass over white, with no shadow.
- **Do** let what the player reaches for with the pointer show where it comes from, in one exponential ease-out from a first frame already readable, and honour reduced motion.

### Don't:
- **Don't** return to the dark dev-tools card with a stats table on top.
- **Don't** use orange, or the outlined title lettering, for anything other than their single roles.
- **Don't** add small labels or kickers above content beyond the Street Name in the title panel; a label belongs only to the field or figure it names.
- **Don't** let a click on the overlay take the keyboard from the game outside the search view, nor one on the Settings window outside a Change and the Deal Margin field.
- **Don't** move what the game changes beyond a cross-fade of 150ms or less, and never loop, pulse, overshoot or time a motion. On the site, motion moves only with the scroll, or answers a click (a Map Leaderboard's band switch): never on arrival.
- **Don't** float any window on a blurred drop shadow; depth is highlights, bevels and hairlines.
- **Don't** introduce a second typeface or icon fonts.
