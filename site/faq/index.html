Safety & your account

The risk to your account, what Minerva looks at, and what it downloads and keeps.

Can I get banned for using Minerva?

It's very unlikely. Minerva only looks at the screen, the way a screenshot does: it never touches the game's memory or files, and never presses keys or clicks for you. Other tools read the game's screen the same way.

Still, Minerva is a third-party tool, not made or approved by Nexon, so there's no guarantee: using it is at your own risk.

Does Minerva read or change the game?

No. Like a screenshot, it captures only what it needs: the minimap title, the bar at the bottom of the game (your EXP, level and name), the tooltip by the pointer when you press an item hotkey, and, while the Deal Finder is on, the game window a few times a second to find a Player Shop. It never reads or changes the game's memory or files, never injects anything into it, and never presses keys or clicks for you.

Are Minerva's hotkeys a keyboard hook?

No. They're ordinary hotkeys registered with Windows, and they act only while the game or one of Minerva's windows is in front. The game never sees them, and in any other app the same keys do what that app makes them do.

Can I check what Minerva does for myself?

Yes. You don't have to take any of this on trust: its source code is public on GitHub, for anyone to read. It's under the MIT licence, so anyone may reuse it, though not the name Minerva or its owl.

Is Minerva free? Does it need an account?

It's free, and you never need to sign up. There's no analytics, and its check for a new version sends only the version you have (see how). Putting a character on the Map Leaderboards needs no account either: a secret key Minerva makes on your PC ties the name to it (see what the site shows). You can link your PCs to Discord from Minerva, so your names follow you to a new PC, but you never have to.

Who makes Minerva?

@egberticus. Ask about Minerva, or say what you'd like it to do, on Minerva's Discord. For something wrong, save a Bug Report instead: it shows what Minerva read.

What does Minerva download, and what does it send?
Map and mob data
Five files from OSMS Data Explorer, under 1 MB together, when you start Minerva if its copy is a day old or an earlier version got it, every 24 hours while it runs, and when you refresh it.
Item data
Two more OSMS files, about 1.2 MB together, on the same schedule.
Pictures
The shown map's picture and its mobs' animations, once, kept until they go 30 days unused. An item's picture only when a card lists it to pick from.
New versions
A small file from GitHub naming the newest release, when you start Minerva, every 6 hours while it runs, and when you ask. See how it updates.

Item Lookup also asks NiaMeowDB's search for an item's name when you press its hotkey or click an Item Card's name: each name once a week at most.

It sends only the version you have with each request, plus the bests of each character you share on the Map Leaderboards (new characters are shared by default): their name, job, level, EXP per hour and dates, with your Leaderboard Key, which the site keeps only as a hash. Turn Share off under a character's name in your Personal Bests and nothing of theirs is sent.

What does Minerva keep on my PC?
  • Your settings (placement, folded sections, hotkeys, a hand-picked game window and the rest of Settings) in %APPDATA%\com.itechify.minerva\settings.json.
  • Beside them: your Personal Bests, each character's best visits on each map, in personal-bests.json; the market prices you keep, in market-prices.json; and the 10 newest Bug Reports you save, in Bug Reports.
  • Once you put a character on the Map Leaderboards, also beside them: the secret Leaderboard Key that ties their name to you, in leaderboard-key. Copy it across when you move to a new PC (or link Discord), and keep it to yourself.
  • The map and item data, pictures and animations in %LOCALAPPDATA%\com.itechify.minerva\cache.

Install & updates

What you need, which file to download, and how to update.

What do I need to run Minerva?

Windows 10 or 11, 64-bit, and MapleStory Classic World played windowed or borderless, with the minimap showing, open or collapsed, anywhere in the game window.

Minerva has been tested mostly with the game windowed at 1920×1200, its UI size set to Default Ratio. Other resolutions and UI sizes should work: if something reads wrong at yours, send a Bug Report.

Does Minerva work with the game fullscreen?

No. Screen capture can't see exclusive fullscreen, so play the game windowed or borderless.

Which file should I download?
Minerva_@VERSION@_x64-setup.exe Recommended
Installs for your user, over earlier versions, with a Start menu entry and an uninstaller.
Minerva_@VERSION@_x64_en-US.msi
The same, as an MSI package. It installs for all users, so it asks for admin rights.
minerva-@VERSION@-portable.exe
No install: run it from any folder. It needs the Microsoft Edge WebView2 runtime, which Windows 11 already has.

The MSI and portable files are on the release's page.

Why does Windows say it "protected your PC"?

Minerva's files aren't code-signed, so Windows SmartScreen doesn't know them yet. Choose More info, then Run anyway.

How do I update Minerva?

From inside Minerva. When a new version is out (see how it checks), click its owl in the notification area, by the clock, and choose Update to the new version, or press Update in Help & Setup's About & Credits. Minerva downloads it, installs it with a small progress window, and starts again. Your settings, hotkeys and Personal Bests carry over. Each release says what's new.

The portable .exe doesn't update itself: its owl's menu opens the new version's release page instead. Quit the running Minerva first (choose Quit Minerva in the owl's menu), then download the new portable .exe and run it. Your settings and Personal Bests carry over.

Does Minerva update itself?

Not without asking you. Minerva checks GitHub for a new version when it starts, every 6 hours while it runs, and when you press Check for updates in Help & Setup's About & Credits. The check sends only the version you have. When one is out, the owl says so at the top centre of the game window until you click it or hide it, and again the next time Minerva starts (see why), and the owl in the notification area and Help & Setup say so too, with a What's new link. Nothing downloads until you choose to update. Later just leaves the notice where it is.

When you update, Minerva downloads the new version, checks it's signed as Minerva's own, installs it with a small progress window and starts again. If you installed the .msi, Windows asks for admin rights first. If the download fails or isn't signed as Minerva's own, Help & Setup says so and nothing is installed: try again, or download it from the release's page. If the installer fails, it says why, and you keep the version you have. Minerva only ever installs the version it told you about, and never an older one.

Updating restarts Minerva, so if you're grinding it asks first: "Updating restarts Minerva and ends this Session." Choose Update now, or Update when I quit to install it the next time you choose Quit Minerva, without restarting. If it's still downloading when you quit, the owl says it's finishing first; Quit now, without updating skips it. Your Personal Bests are saved either way.

The portable .exe never installs anything: it says the new version is out, with a link to its release page (see how to update it).

To stop the checks, turn off Check for updates in Settings, the gear at the top of the panel. The button in Help & Setup still checks when you press it.

Why is an owl talking to me over the game?

That's Minerva. At the top centre of the game window, clear of what Minerva reads, the owl says what came of an Item Lookup, and that a new version is out. Click its bubble to open Help & Setup's About & Credits, where you update, or its × to hide it until Minerva starts again; choosing to update hides it too. It doesn't move or make a sound, so you can leave it be while you play. A new version you found with Check for updates shows only in Help & Setup, where you're already looking, and with Check for updates turned off in Settings the owl says nothing of new versions.

Setup & hotkeys

The first run, finding your map, and changing the hotkeys.

Setup

What happens the first time I start Minerva?

A setup window walks you through:

  1. The account warning.
  2. Finding the game window, or picking it by hand.
  3. Setting up the game: windowed or borderless, with the minimap showing, open or collapsed.
  4. Finding your map: Minerva finds the minimap title by itself and shows the map it names.
  5. The Grind Tracker, which needs nothing marked.

Finish starts the overlay. Closing setup on its first run quits.

Do I have to mark the EXP bar?

No. Minerva finds the EXP bar in the game's bar at the bottom of the window by itself, and reads the Grind Tracker's figures from it, wherever the game window is, whatever its size, and at any of the game's UI Size settings. A mark you made in an earlier version isn't used any more. To turn the Grind Tracker off, or on again, use its switch in Settings.

How do I open setup again?

Ctrl+Shift+S, or Help & Setup on the panel or on the owl in the notification area. There you can see the map Minerva found, or read the Overlay Guide, which lists your hotkeys and explains placement and the hunting figures. About & Credits, beside it, links back to this site.

Where is Minerva while it runs, and how do I quit it?

Its owl sits in the notification area, the system tray by the clock. Click it for Help & Setup, Bug Report or Quit Minerva. That's the only way to quit, whether or not the game is open. Only one Minerva runs at a time: starting it again brings up the one that's running.

Hotkeys

How do I change a hotkey?
  1. Click the gear at the top of the panel, or Change hotkeys in setup's Overlay Guide. Settings opens in a window of its own beside the panel: drag it anywhere, and close it with its × or the gear.
  2. Click the hotkey's Change.
  3. Press the new combination, or Esc to cancel. While Settings waits, the hotkeys are released, so your keys reach it rather than acting.

A combination must hold Ctrl, Alt or Win. Ctrl+Shift with a key is safest: MapleStory attacks with Ctrl and jumps with Alt by default.

What if the keys I want are already taken?

If another Minerva hotkey has them, Swap trades the two. If Windows or another app has them, the panel and setup say which, and Settings marks the row.

Can I turn a hotkey off?

Yes, so it stops taking its keys from the game: choose Turn off, or press Backspace or Delete while it waits for keys. Its button at the top of the panel still works. The Item Card and Item Lookup have no button, so they can't be used while theirs is off.

Can I undo a change to the hotkeys?

Every change works at once and is saved. Restore defaults puts every hotkey back, and on, and Undo brings yours back until you change another or close Settings.

The panel & the map

Moving and placing the panel, the Map Preview and its Hidden Portals, the mobs, and maps that share a title.

The panel

What does the panel show?

The map you're on, by its name and its Map Mark, then three sections: the Map, Navigation while you have a Hunting Target or a Destination, and the Mobs. Click a section's heading to fold it, and again to open it. A folded section stays folded from map to map, and the next time you start Minerva.

What's the emblem beside the map's name?

Its Map Mark, the emblem the minimap shows beside its title for the area the map is in, like Henesys's shield, Kerning City's stone or Sleepywood's skull. Every other map the panel names, such as a portal's destination or a stop on your route, has a smaller one, so you can tell where it is.

What do the panel's buttons do?
  • Search for a mob to hunt, a map to go to, or an item.
  • The next map with the same title, only when maps share it.
  • Show or hide the Grind Tracker, when you track your EXP.
  • Change the placement.
  • Settings, to change the hotkeys or refresh the game data.
  • Help & Setup.
  • Fold the panel to an icon: see below.

Rest the pointer on a button to see its hotkey. Click the map's name to open its MeowDB page.

Does clicking the panel take the keyboard from the game?

No. Click it whenever you like: the game keeps the keyboard. Only the search view takes it while it's open, and Settings as it opens and while you change a hotkey or type the Deal Margin. Click back into the game and the keys are the game's again: the search view closes, and Settings stays open.

How do I move or resize the panel?

Drag it by its header to move it, or by a side or a bottom corner to make it wider or narrower. Its height follows what it shows, and a long list scrolls instead. Drop it on the minimap title and it moves just clear, so Minerva can still read the title.

Can I put the panel outside the game, or on another monitor?

Yes. Ctrl+Shift+P switches between four placements:

  • Inside: it keeps the spot you dragged it to in the game window. It starts on the window's right edge, a third of the way down.
  • Docked right or left: it follows the game window's outside edge, and goes inside when there's no room on screen.
  • Detached: it stays wherever you drag it, on any monitor, with a taskbar entry of its own.
Why does the panel disappear?

It shows on top of everything, but only while the game or one of Minerva's windows is in front. While another app is, or the game is minimised, it hides: opening a MeowDB page in your browser hides it until you go back to the game.

Does the panel show up in screenshots and streams?

Yes. It's a window of its own over the game, so whatever captures your screen captures it too.

Can I fold the panel out of the way?

Yes. Ctrl+Shift+C, or its minus button, folds it to an icon that shows the map's Hidden Portal count, with an orange dot when something needs a look. Click the icon to open the panel again.

The Map and its Hidden Portals

What's a Hidden Portal?

A way to another map that the game doesn't show. The Map Preview rings it dashed, and lists it under the picture with where it leads, tagged Hidden Street when it leads to one, or Quest when it only works during a quest.

What do the rings' colours mean?
  • Blue: to another map.
  • Green: within the map, with a dashed arrow to where it lands.
  • Grey: to a map Minerva doesn't know.
  • Wide magenta: the next portal on your way to a Hunting Target.

A dashed ring is a portal the game doesn't show.

Can I zoom in on the map?

Yes. Turn the mouse wheel over the picture to zoom in on the part you point at, as far as the map's own size. The rings keep their size, so portals that crowd together come apart. Drag the picture to move around it, and double-click it to see the whole map again. A new map always shows whole.

How do I see where a portal leads?

Rest the pointer on its ring, or on its row under the picture. A card shows the spot up close, so you can tell which ledge a hidden one is on, then says where it leads, its street and its kind. The ring and its row light together.

Why aren't a town's doors listed as Hidden Portals?

You can see them in the game, so they're ringed like any visible portal rather than listed. A teleport within the map is never listed either, though an invisible one is dashed.

Why is the map's picture missing?

It may still be loading ("Loading map…"), or it couldn't be fetched ("Couldn't load the map picture"), and the Hidden Portals list without it. When maps share a title, it waits until it's settled which one you're on. A map with no picture and no Hidden Portals has no Map section.

Mobs

What are the EXP/h and Mobs/h figures?

The map's Map Summary estimates its hourly ceiling, assuming mobs are killed as they respawn. For each mob, multiply its spawn points by its spawns per hour, then by its base EXP. Normal spawns use 3,600 ÷ 7.56 per point per hour for a party of 6 or more, and 75% of that solo (about 357). Timed spawns use 3,600 ÷ their timer in seconds for either party size; a ranged timer uses OSMS's listed average. Round each mob's result, then add them for the map. Mobs/h uses the same calculation without multiplying by EXP. EXP buffs, events and the map's Mob Rate aren't applied.

Hover the ? beside solo EXP/h in the panel, Grind Tracker or Personal Bests to see the calculation there too. Each mob's Share is its share of all the map's spawn points: the higher it is, the fewer other mobs in your way.

Where do a map's mobs spawn?

Every red dot on the Map Preview is a spawn point, as the minimap dots mobs. Rest the pointer on a mob's row to light its spawn points and fade the rest, or on a dot to see which mob spawns there, close up.

How do I see more about a mob?

Rest the pointer on its name for its Mob Card: the mob as OSMS animates it, and what its row doesn't show, such as its element, touch damage, accuracy, avoid and defences. Click the name to open the mob's MeowDB page.

Can I shorten a long mob list?

Click the Mobs heading to fold the section to its heading and the count, on every map, until you open it again.

Maps that share a title

What happens when two maps share a title?

A few do, like the two Maple Road / Mushroom Town maps. If they'd show the same summary, mobs and Hidden Portals anyway, the panel just shows them, without a picture. Otherwise it picks the one connected to the map you came from. Failing that, it shows the first, marked Not sure.

The panel says "Not sure". How do I pick the right map?

It lists the maps it could be, each with its first mobs, mob levels and Hidden Portal count. Ctrl+Shift+N, or the arrows button, steps through them, and your pick holds until you change maps.

What about maps whose names differ only by a numeral?

Like Rocky Road I, II and III: when Minerva can't make the numeral out, it picks the one the map you came from leads to, if that leads to just one of them. Otherwise it says it isn't sure rather than guess.

Hunting

Searching, choosing a map to hunt on, and following the way there.

How do I search?

Ctrl+Shift+F, or the magnifier at the top of the panel. Type part of a name: the mobs, maps and items that have it show in a section each, mobs first, names that start with what you typed first. Each section shows its best five and counts the rest. Pick one with the arrow keys and Enter, or a click.

What happens when I pick a mob, a map or an item?
  • A mob: it lists the maps the mob spawns on, to pick one to hunt it on.
  • A map: it becomes your Destination, and the panel shows the way there.
  • An item: its Item Card opens beside the panel.
How are a mob's maps ranked?

By Density Tier, then the nearest within a tier, each with its figures. Tick Taxis or Teleport Scrolls to count them as ways to travel. Event maps and the developers' room aren't listed, nor found as maps.

What do Density, Share, Tier and Distance mean?
Density
How many of the mob you can kill there: its spawns, times how fast the map refills.
Share
The mob's share of all the map's spawns: the higher, the fewer other mobs in your way.
Tier
The map's Density Tier: High, Medium or Low against the mob's densest map.
Distance
The fewest map changes to get there.
What's the difference between a Hunting Target and a Destination?

Pick one of a mob's maps and it's your Hunting Target: Navigation shows the mob as OSMS animates it, the map, its figures and the way there. Pick a map itself and it's your Destination: the map, its street, how far it is and the way, without a mob. You have one or the other: the last you picked.

How does Navigation show the way?

Between the Map and the Mobs, it lists the route from the map you're on, worked out again as you move, and the portal it takes next wears a wide magenta ring on the Map Preview. When you arrive, it says You're here, with how far you came, until you leave the map.

How do I change or clear my Hunting Target?

Its magnifier opens the mob's map list again, to pick another map, and the × clears a Hunting Target or a Destination. Otherwise it lasts until you close Minerva.

Why can't I move my character while searching?

The search view has the keyboard, for your typing, until it closes. Esc or the back arrow goes back a step without changing anything: from a mob's maps to what you typed, and from there to the panel, where the game has the keyboard again.

Grinding

The Grind Tracker, its Session, and your Personal Bests.

The Grind Tracker

What does the Grind Tracker show?
  • Time to Level, at your current pace.
  • Your EXP per hour over the last 5 minutes and, once you've played longer, over the whole Session.
  • After a minute on a map, how your pace compares with its solo EXP/h ceiling.
  • Your deaths, and what they cost.
  • Your last 5 minutes, kill by kill, raced against the 5 minutes before and, when you come close, your Personal Best.
  • At its foot, the EXP bar as it last read it, so you can see it's being read, with the character playing beside it. After a death, the hatched stretch is the ground it took back, until you're past it again.
  • Under the EXP bar, where it will be in so long at your pace, and Time to Level at its end.
How do I turn the Grind Tracker on?

It's on from the start, in a window of its own beside the panel: Minerva finds your EXP bar by itself. Ctrl+Shift+G or the panel's button shows or hides it, and its × closes the window while the Session carries on. To turn it off, click the gear at the top of the panel and untick Grind Tracker: Minerva then doesn't read your EXP at all, until you tick it again.

How do I reset the Session?

The circular arrow beside Time to Level resets it, and a new one starts at your next EXP gain; until then, Undo brings the old one back. It has no hotkey, so a stray key press while you fight can't clear it. Switch character and the Session starts afresh by itself.

How does Minerva know which character I'm playing?

From the status bar at the bottom of the game window, with nothing to set up: it reads your level, job and name there every second, and the Grind Tracker shows your name and level beside your EXP bar. Log in as another character and it reads the new name, so the Session starts afresh within seconds. A level-up the status bar shows counts at once, even if something covered your EXP bar as it filled. While it can't read a name, it still tells another character from the EXP bar.

Can I move the Grind Tracker?

Yes. It sits beside the panel with their tops level. Drag it by its bar anywhere, even outside the game window, and it keeps that spot; drag it back beside the panel and it follows the panel again. Like the panel, it hides while another app is in front or the game is minimised.

Personal Bests

What's a Personal Best?

On a map with mobs, the best EXP per hour the character you're playing has had over 5 minutes of play there. Come back to the map and a Best row shows it from the moment you arrive. Beat it, or play 5 minutes on a map for the first time, and the row moves up to your new best at once, saying "this visit". A yellow star marks your last 5 minutes while they're that best. When you slow down, the row keeps your best, so you can see where you were.

What's the line under my rates?

Your last 5 minutes raced against the 5 minutes before them. The flat line is your earlier pace. Your line sinks between kills and steps up with each one: level means you're keeping pace, sinking means you've slowed, and a cliff is a death. The number by your dot is how much EXP you're ahead or behind. It appears once you've played 6 minutes.

On a map where you have a Personal Best, once you come within 10% of its pace, it joins as a gold line ending in a star and stays until you leave the map. Get above it and the gap is lit yellow; the number by your dot turns gold and counts against your best. Set a new best and the gold line moves up to it, so later on it shows how far you are from your best pace.

Are bests kept for each character?

Yes. Minerva reads your character's name, level and job from the game's status bar, keeps each character's bests apart, and notes the level and job you had when you set each one. The Grind Tracker races the best of the character you're playing.

Bests from before Minerva knew your characters, or set while it couldn't read the name, are Unassigned, and the Grind Tracker races those while no name is read. To hand them out, pick Unassigned in your Personal Bests, then Assign all to… and a character, or a visit's Move to move just that one, and click again to confirm. Every visit has a Move, in case Minerva misread the name when it was set.

Renamed a character? The new name shows up as a new character. Pick it, click Manage, choose Merge into… and the old name, and click again to confirm: its bests move across. To remove a character and all their bests, pick them, click Manage, then Remove twice. Neither can be undone, and both take the character off the Map Leaderboards. A best on a map that's no longer in the map list stays, marked "Not in the map list".

Where can I see my best on every map?

Click the star on the Grind Tracker's bar to open your Personal Bests. It lists your best on every map for the character you're playing, highest first, with the map's mob levels, how close you came to its solo EXP/h ceiling (the ? explains it) and when you set it. Switch to another character, or to Unassigned, at the top. Each best is a bar on the same scale; a notch is another of your visits there, and the dot is the visit you're on, moving as you play. Click a column to sort by it, or a map to open it on MeowDB.

Can I see each of my visits to a map?

A map keeps each character's 10 best visits, and their best in each Level Band even if it isn't among them, so levelling up never loses your best at lower levels. In your Personal Bests, click its count ("3 visits") to list them, best first, each with the level you set it at, its EXP per hour and its own bar, its share of the ceiling and its date, then a link to the map's Map Leaderboard. The visit you're on is marked "This visit", and joins your history when you leave the map; until then it can be removed, but not moved.

A best came from a misreading. How do I remove it?

On the Grind Tracker, the × beside the Best row removes it at once, with no undo, whether you set it on this visit or an earlier one, and your next-best visit takes over. In your Personal Bests, click a visit's ×, then "Remove?" to confirm, the visit you're on included. Once this visit's best is removed, only the 5 minutes after it count towards a new one, so the misreading isn't counted again.

Can I move the Personal Bests window?

Yes. It opens in the middle of the game; drag it by its bar and it opens where you left it next time. It keeps up as you play, shows and hides with the panel, and closes with the star or its ×.

Map Leaderboards

Players' Personal Bests side by side on this site: what they rank, Level Bands, putting your character there or taking them off, and reporting an entry.

What are the Map Leaderboards?

A page on this site for each map, ranking players' Personal Bests from Minerva, so you can see what EXP per hour others at your level get there. Each Level Band is ranked separately and shows its top 50, with each character's name, job, level, EXP per hour and date, and a bar showing how far behind #1 they are. On the Map Leaderboards page, Where to train lists the maps for your Level Band, you can find a map by its name, and Latest lists the newest bests on every map. Click a character's name to see all their bests.

How does Where to train pick a Level Band's maps?

Pick your Level Band on the Map Leaderboards page, and it lists each map whose mobs are at those levels, and any map someone has an entry on in that band. A map's mobs are the middle half of its spawns, so one stray mob far above or below the rest doesn't put it in another band. Beside each map are those mobs' levels, the band's best EXP per hour there and who set it, and how many entries it has. The maps with entries come first, the best first, and a band with more than 20 maps shows its first 15: Show all lists the rest. Pick an area, by the same emblem the game's minimap shows beside the map's name, to see only its maps.

What's a Level Band?

Ten levels of a Map Leaderboard: 1–9, 10–19, 20–29 and on, so you're ranked against players near your level. An entry is one character's best visit to the map at those levels, so a character that levels up can have an entry in each band. The highest EXP per hour ranks first; on a tie, whoever set it first.

What does the EXP per hour on the Map Leaderboards count?

The Personal Best as Minerva counted it: everything the EXP bar gained, at its best over 5 minutes of play on the map. It's raw, so party play, EXP events and buffs aren't told apart from playing solo. Only a figure over 5 times the map's solo EXP/h ceiling is kept off, as one no map gives.

How do I put my character on the Map Leaderboards?

A new character is on them already. Share, the switch under their name in your Personal Bests, starts on for every character Minerva meets for the first time. A character Minerva knew before sharing was on by default starts off: pick them and click Share to turn it on. Minerva uploads the character's best visit on each map in each Level Band, then each new best when its visit ends. Removing, moving or merging visits updates the site too.

Unassigned bests are never uploaded, and nor are bests set before Minerva recorded the date, level and job; a character with only those has no switch yet. Beside the switch, Minerva says when the last upload went through. If one fails, because you're offline for instance, a note above your bests says so and Minerva retries by itself; Send again retries straight away.

What does the site show of me?

For each entry: your character's name and job, the exact level they set it at, the EXP per hour, and the date, for everyone to see. Nothing else about you or your PC goes, and you need no account. Instead Minerva makes a secret Leaderboard Key on your PC the first time it uploads, which ties your character's name to it, so no one else can upload under that name. The site keeps only a scrambled form (a hash) of the key, and you never need to see it. The newest bests on every map are also listed in Latest on the Map Leaderboards page, each with its map. Taking a map's top spot off someone else is marked there, and posted in Minerva's Discord.

Can others see all my character's bests in one place?

Yes. Click a character's name anywhere on the Map Leaderboards and their page lists every entry they have, a Level Band at a time, each with its rank and a link to it on its map. Over them are their job and level as their newest best says, how many #1s and top-3 places they hold, how many maps they're on, and their best EXP per hour. Each Level Band of a map counts on its own, however few entries it has. It shows only what their entries already show map by map, and nothing ties it to your other characters or your Discord. Taking the character off takes their page with them.

My character's name turned up in Minerva's Discord. Why?

When one of your uploads takes a map's top spot off someone else, Minerva's Discord posts it in #map-leaderboards: the map, by its name and its Map Mark if it has one, and your character's name, the Level Band, the EXP per hour and whose spot it was. That's all from the entry itself, so it says nothing the Map Leaderboards don't already show, and it never pings anyone. Only a spot taken is posted: the first entry in a Level Band nobody had one in, and beating your own best, both go unsaid. Taking your character off stops it, since a character that isn't on the Map Leaderboards is never posted.

How do I take my character off the Map Leaderboards?

Click Share under the character's name in your Personal Bests to turn it off: every entry of the character is deleted from the site, and it stays off. Removing the character from your Personal Bests, or merging them into another, takes them off too. The name stays yours, so you can put them back any time. A single best that came from a bug goes when you remove that visit.

It says another Minerva holds my character's name. What now?

A name belongs to the Leaderboard Key that uploaded it first. If that was you, on a PC you've since reinstalled or replaced, your old key went with it. If your old PC was linked to Discord, click link this one on the note in your Personal Bests and link this PC to the same Discord: your uploads go through again by themselves. Otherwise, ask on Minerva's Discord, with the character's name: once Minerva has tried to upload from your new PC, the maintainer can hand the name to its key. Then click Send again on the note, and your uploads go through again. To keep your names when you move PCs, link Discord, or copy leaderboard-key across (where Minerva keeps its files).

What does Link Discord do?

It links this PC to your Discord on owlofminerva.app, so your characters' names on the Map Leaderboards follow you to a new PC. In your Personal Bests, beside a character's Share, click Link Discord: your browser opens the site, which signs you in with Discord and asks before it links this PC. Then on a new PC, or after reinstalling Windows, link that one to the same Discord, and it can upload your characters again, with no need to ask anyone. Your Personal Bests then say Linked to Discord as your username.

The site keeps your Discord user ID and username, and shows neither. It's optional: Minerva uploads just the same without it. At your account on the site you can unlink a PC (one you sold, say) or delete the account; your characters stay on the Map Leaderboards either way. Link before you need it: a PC that was never linked can't hand its names on, and then the maintainer does it by hand.

Where's my character's entry?

In your Personal Bests, click a map's count of visits: under its visits, Map Leaderboard opens the map's page at the Level Band of the character you're playing, marks your entry and says its rank, even outside the top 50. If it says your character has no entry there, they haven't put a best there yet, or the entry has gone: taken off the Map Leaderboards, or removed after a report. If the site kept one of your bests off, as over 5 times the map's solo EXP/h or removed after a report, a note above your bests says so.

An entry can't be right. What can I do?

Report it: each entry has a flag, Report, at the end of its row. Say what's wrong if you like, and send it. Minerva's maker reads the reports, and removes an entry that can't be right. Your own entry, the one your Map Leaderboard link stars, has no Report: to take it off, see taking your character off.

Items

The Item Card, the market prices you keep, Item Lookup and the Deal Finder.

The Item Card

How do I open an Item Card?

Hover an item in the game until its tooltip shows, then press Ctrl+Shift+I. Minerva reads the name in the tooltip nearest the pointer, the inventory's or any window's drawn the same way, and shows the card at the pointer. Or find the item in search and pick it: its card opens beside the panel.

What does "Shop pays" mean?

What an NPC shop pays for one. When a shop won't buy the item, the card says so, as it does for a Cash item. It's not known for scrolls, starter gear and arrows, which the game's data gives no price for, nor for throwing stars and bullets until a sale in the game confirms what a shop pays.

Where do the market prices come from?

From you. Minerva reads no market prices of its own, so you keep them for the items you care about. The button by Market opens MesoWatch, where you search the item to see what players pay: MesoWatch has no page for each item to open.

How do I set a market price?

Click a price, or "Not set", and type it as players write it: 450,000, 450k or 1.2m. Enter keeps it, Esc leaves it as it was, and an empty field clears it. It's saved with the day you set it, which its tooltip shows, and the next card for that item shows it.

Can I keep more than one price for each item?

Yes. Click the + by Market and name a new line, such as the cheapest one listed beside what it typically sells for: every card lists it from then on, with its own price for each item. Click a line's name to rename it, or to remove it with the bin beside it; the card asks first, saying how many items' prices go with it.

Why does the card ask me to pick an item?

Some items that don't look alike share a name: Blue Moon is a top and an earring. The card lists them with their pictures and kinds: click the one your tooltip shows.

How do I move or close an Item Card?

Drag it by its bar: it stays there until it closes, and the next card opens at the pointer again. Close it with its ×, or press Ctrl+Shift+I again: over another item for that item's card, anywhere else to close it. One card shows at a time, and clicks on it never reach the game.

Does typing a price take the keyboard from the game?

While you type a price or a line's name, the card has the keyboard. Clicking back into the game gives it back, keeping what you typed.

Why didn't a card show?

The owl at the top centre of the game window says why: no tooltip by the pointer, a name it couldn't read, a Minerva window covering the name, no item of that name, or the item data not loaded yet.

Item Lookup

Which name does Item Lookup read?

The one on the tooltip's line with the blue dot, so a crafted item's maker isn't taken for it. It opens a page only when MeowDB has an item of that name, allowing for letters the reading confuses, such as I and l. Clicking an Item Card's name opens the same page.

What does the owl say after Item Lookup?

What came of it, in a speech bubble at the top centre of the game window: the page it opened, or why nothing opened: no tooltip by the pointer, a name it couldn't read, a Minerva window covering the name, no MeowDB item of that name, or MeowDB out of reach. It goes after a few seconds, and clicks pass through it to the game.

What if several MeowDB items share the name?

The owl lists them with their categories for you to pick, and stays half a minute. From an Item Card's name, the one of the card's kind opens if MeowDB has just one.

Can I use the Item Card or Item Lookup without its hotkey?

No: they have no button, so turning off their hotkey turns them off. Search still opens any item's card, and its name still opens its MeowDB page.

The Deal Finder

How do I find the deals in a Player Shop?

Press Ctrl+Shift+D, or click the price tag at the top of the panel, and open a shop. A badge shows to the left of each listing and follows as you scroll. It stays on until you press the key or click the tag again, or press Turn off in the panel. It starts off each time you open Minerva, and while it's on, the folded panel's owl holds a magnifying glass.

What does the Deal Finder compare prices with?

One of your market lines, the Deal Line: on any Item Card, the line with the filled ring. Click another line's ring to compare with that one instead. A listing of several is judged by the price of one: 10 for 6,800 mesos is 680 each. Throwing stars and bullets are judged by the listing's whole price, since its number is how many the set holds. To count only bigger savings, set the Deal Margin in Settings, the gear at the top of the panel: at 10%, a deal is at least 10% below the line.

What do the Deal Finder's badges mean?

A coloured badge is a deal. It says how far under the Deal Line the listing is, and the line's price, such as −32% · 1,470. The colour grades it the way the game grades items: blue for a good deal, purple for a great one (20% or more under), gold for a steal (40% or more). A pale badge isn't a deal, and says how far over it is, such as +12%. The bar along each badge's bottom edge shows the price against the Deal Line, the notch in the middle, so you can spot the biggest deals down the column. Click any badge to open the item's card.

no price means the Deal Line has no price for the item: click it to set one. ? means Minerva couldn't read the listing for sure, such as a name the shop cut short that could be several items, and it won't guess. A scroll cut short is usually named anyway, by the colour of its picture, which tells Lesser, Intermediate, Greater and Chaos apart. For the rest, hover the listing until the game shows its tooltip, and the badge judges the item the tooltip names until the list changes. While the pointer or a tooltip covers a listing, its badge fades and keeps its last reading, with an orange dot, until the list changes. Equipment is judged by its name alone, as the shop doesn't show its stats.

Does the Deal Finder read the whole shop?

It reads the listings on screen, and you scroll for the rest: Minerva never clicks or scrolls for you. Sold-out listings get no badge. It finds the shop wherever its window is, so you can drag it, at any window size. It reads best with the game's UI size set to Default Ratio: with Ideal Ratio, a listing now and then reads less reliably. It reads Player Shops; Hired Merchants aren't read yet.

Can I use the Deal Finder without its hotkey?

Yes: the price tag at the top of the panel turns it on and off, as its hotkey does, and stays pressed while it's on. Turn off its hotkey in Settings, and the tag still works.

Troubleshooting & Bug Reports

What to do when it doesn't work, and how to report what's wrong.

When it doesn't work

Setup can't find the game window. What do I do?

Start MapleStory first, or pick its window by hand: choose Select game window, then hold the pointer over the game while setup counts down.

The panel says "Can't find the minimap's title". What now?

No minimap title has been found for 10 seconds, so the panel keeps the last map it showed. Open the minimap, or expand it if you collapsed it to its bar, and keep it out from under other windows: Minerva finds it wherever it is, within a second or two. If the minimap is showing and the panel still can't find it, send a Bug Report.

Why doesn't the panel show?

It shows only while the game is open and not minimised, and the game or one of Minerva's windows is in front: click into the game. Screen capture can't see exclusive fullscreen, so play windowed or borderless.

Why can't the panel tell which map I'm on?

It says "Reading the minimap title…", or shows the last map it read marked Not sure. Keep the minimap's title in sight, open or collapsed, with nothing over it: the pointer or another window over it makes it unreadable. If it goes on with the title in plain sight, send a Bug Report: Minerva reads best at the game's Default Ratio UI size.

What does "Map recognition unavailable" mean?

Minerva couldn't load the map list from OSMS Data Explorer, which its first launch needs. Check your connection: it tries again every minute. If it says OSMS's data has changed format, Minerva needs an update.

Why does a hotkey do nothing?

Hotkeys act only while the game or one of Minerva's windows is in front. If another app has the keys, the panel says so: close that app, or change the hotkey.

Why does the Grind Tracker say "Can't read the EXP bar"?

Something has covered the bar for ten seconds; it says so rather than count the time as a break. Move whatever covers the bar. If it says it can't find the game's bar at the bottom of the window, though the bar shows, Minerva doesn't know the game at that window size or UI Size yet: send a Bug Report.

Why are my Personal Bests gone?

If their file can't be read, Minerva renames it personal-bests.json.bad and starts with no bests. If it can't rename it, it leaves it as it is and saves no bests until the next launch. The file is beside your settings.

A character you show on the Map Leaderboards keeps their entries there: Minerva sends nothing of theirs, and a note in your Personal Bests says so, since what's left on your PC would replace them. Click Send what's here on it, and again to confirm, to send what's there now instead.

Search doesn't find a new map, mob or item. What do I do?

Minerva gets the game's data once a day, so a patch can take a day to show. To get it now, open Settings, the gear at the top of the panel, and press Refresh under Game Data. A new version of Minerva gets it afresh the first time it starts.

If Refresh says to update Minerva, OSMS Data Explorer's data changed in a way this version can't read: install the latest release. Until then, Minerva keeps the data it has.

Bug Reports

How do I report a bug?

When Minerva shows something wrong, like a rate far higher than you're getting, click its owl in the notification area and choose Bug Report right away. It saves a .zip, shows it in its folder, and opens the bug form on GitHub. Drag the file into the form, and say what looked wrong and about when.

No GitHub account? Send the file to @egberticus in a direct message on Minerva's Discord, not in its channels: its pictures can show other players' names and chat.

What's in a Bug Report?

What Minerva read and made of it since you started it, up to a day back:

  • Each reading of the minimap title and the game's bar at the bottom of the window, with pictures of the title, the EXP figures, and the level, job and name on that bar; each item you looked up or opened a card for, with a picture of the game around the pointer (your inventory, and whatever is beside it); and, while the Deal Finder was on, pictures of the Player Shop's list. Nothing else on your screen, but for what's below.
  • If the Deal Finder couldn't find the shop, a picture of the whole game window, chat and other players' names included; and if Minerva couldn't find the game's bar at the bottom, a picture of the strip across the bottom of the window, which can show chat. One of each at most, from the last 10 minutes, so the bug can be seen.
  • The map it showed, the Grind Tracker's figures, and why they changed: a level-up, a death, a misread set aside.
  • What you did in Minerva: its buttons, hotkeys and searches.
  • Your settings and Personal Bests, which characters you show on the Map Leaderboards and what the site answered (never your Leaderboard Key), Minerva's version, your Windows version and display scaling, and any errors it ran into.
Is a Bug Report sent anywhere?

No. It stays on your PC, in %APPDATA%\com.itechify.minerva\Bug Reports, until you attach it to an issue. Saving one changes nothing in Minerva, which keeps the 10 newest. With it, the bug can be played back as it happened instead of guessed at.

Something else is wrong. What do I do?

Save a Bug Report and report it on GitHub: what you did, what you expected, and what Minerva showed.
